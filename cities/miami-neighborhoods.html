MIAMI · NEIGHBORHOODS

Which part of Miami actually fits your trip.

Miami is not one city to explore—it is several, pushed together. Pick your neighborhoods before you pick your hotel or your restaurants.

Every Miami trip goes better when you stop thinking of it as one destination and start thinking of it as a handful of very different neighborhoods that happen to share a coastline. Here is what each one is actually good for, so you can build a trip around the two or three that match what you want.

South Beach

The postcard version of Miami: Art Deco hotels, Ocean Drive, beach access a block from your room and the latest nights in the city. It is touristy in the center and genuinely beautiful at the edges—walk a few blocks off Ocean Drive and the crowds thin out fast. Good for a first visit, a bachelor or bachelorette weekend, or anyone who wants to be able to walk from bed to sand.

Wynwood

Miami's arts district: warehouse-turned-gallery blocks, the Wynwood Walls outdoor mural project, breweries, food halls and a young, design-forward crowd. There is almost no beach access and not much reason to sleep here, but it is one of the best half-days in the city and a strong dinner-and-drinks base.

Design District

A small, walkable pocket of luxury retail, architecture and some of the city's best restaurants, right next to Wynwood. Pair the two into a single afternoon-into-evening block rather than treating them as separate trips.

Little Havana

The center of Cuban Miami: cafecito windows, cigar shops, live music spilling onto the sidewalk on Calle Ocho, and some of the most consistent, unpretentious food in the city. It rewards a slow visit more than a checklist one—budget real time here rather than a drive-through stop.

Brickell

Miami's financial district and, increasingly, its most convenient base: glass towers, polished restaurants, walkable blocks and quick access to Downtown, the Design District and the airport. Less character than Wynwood or Little Havana, more convenience than almost anywhere else in the city.

Coconut Grove & Coral Gables

The quieter, greener, more residential side of Miami. Coconut Grove has a village feel with waterfront parks; Coral Gables has Mediterranean-style architecture, the historic Biltmore, and a genuinely walkable downtown strip. Both are better fits for a slower trip, a family visit, or a day away from the density of the beach.

Key Biscayne

A literal island escape a short drive from Downtown: quieter beaches, state park access and a resort-town pace without leaving Miami-Dade County. Worth a half-day even if you are not staying there.

South Beach + Little Havana

FOOD-FOCUSED

Little Havana + Design District

QUIET & GREEN

Coral Gables + Key Biscayne
