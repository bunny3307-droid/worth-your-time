SEOUL · THINGS TO DO

Palaces, hanbok rentals and Han River evenings.

Seoul's culture and sights go well beyond a single palace photo. Here is what is actually worth the time.

Gyeongbokgung Palace

Seoul's largest and most important royal palace, with a changing-of-the-guard ceremony held several times a day. Rent a hanbok (traditional dress) nearby and you get free palace entry as a bonus—a genuinely popular, worthwhile tradition rather than just a photo prop.

Bukchon Hanok Village

Covered in the neighborhoods guide too: a hillside of preserved traditional houses within walking distance of Gyeongbokgung, best combined into a single morning.

N Seoul Tower

Perched on Namsan Mountain, the tower offers the city's best panoramic view, especially around sunset. The cable car or a hiking trail both get you up; budget extra time if you want to walk.

Dongdaemun Design Plaza

A striking, curved landmark building by architect Zaha Hadid, hosting design exhibitions and markets—worth a visit for the architecture alone, day or night, when it is lit up.

War Memorial of Korea

A large, well-curated museum covering Korean military history, including the Korean War. A serious, worthwhile stop for anyone interested in the country's modern history.

Han River parks

Riverside parks on both banks of the Han River are a genuine local pastime—renting a bike, ordering delivery to a picnic blanket, or catching the Banpo Bridge's evening rainbow fountain show are all standard, well-loved ways to spend an evening.

Day trip: the DMZ

The Korean Demilitarized Zone is one of the most-visited day trips from Seoul, typically booked as a half-day or full-day organized tour due to access restrictions. Book a few days ahead, especially in peak season.
