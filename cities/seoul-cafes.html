SEOUL · CAFÉS

Seoul's café culture is not optional.

Cafés in Seoul are not a break from the itinerary—they are part of it. Here is how to navigate one of the best café scenes in the world.

Seongsu's industrial-cafe scene

Converted warehouses and factories now house some of Seoul's most design-forward cafés—large-format spaces built as much for atmosphere as for coffee. Weekday mornings are dramatically calmer than weekend afternoons.

Ikseon-dong's hanok cafés

Traditional Korean houses converted into small, atmospheric cafés—expect a wait at the most photogenic spots, especially on weekends, and consider a weekday visit instead.

Themed cafés

Seoul has an entire category of destination cafés built around a concept: book cafés for quiet reading, dessert-focused cafés, and small specialty shops built around a single drink or pastry. Treat one as an activity, not just a coffee stop.

Bingsu and dessert culture

Bingsu—shaved ice topped with fruit, red beans or sweet toppings—is a genuine Korean dessert institution, usually meant for sharing between two or more people. It is a good reason to sit down for thirty minutes between activities.

Cafés as workspaces

Cafés in Seoul are commonly used as informal offices and study spaces—free WiFi and long stays are generally welcomed, especially outside of peak weekend hours, which makes them a reliable place to regroup mid-day.

THE WORTH-IT RULE: Save one café stop per day as flexible time rather than pre-planning every one—Seoul's best café finds are often the ones you stumble into between bigger plans.
