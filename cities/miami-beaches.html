MIAMI · BEACHES & OUTDOORS

South Beach is not the only sand in town.

Miami's beaches and green space go well beyond Ocean Drive—here is where to actually put a towel down, and where to get outside.

South Beach

The famous stretch, with lifeguard stands, public access every few blocks and the Art Deco skyline behind it. It is also the most crowded, especially south of about 5th Street. Go early to claim space, or walk further north for more room.

Mid-Beach & North Beach

A short drive or rideshare from South Beach, with noticeably fewer crowds and a more local, residential feel. The same ocean, a fraction of the tourists.

Key Biscayne

Crandon Park and Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park offer some of the calmest, cleanest beach in the area, plus a historic lighthouse and real green space—worth the short drive over the causeway for a slower beach day.

Biscayne Bay

Kayaking, paddleboarding and boat tours through the bay's mangroves and small islands are a good way to see the city from the water without the South Beach crowd. Half-day tours are widely available and do not require experience.

Vizcaya Museum & Gardens

An early-1900s waterfront estate with formal Italian Renaissance-style gardens on Biscayne Bay—part outdoor time, part culture, and one of the most photographed spots in the city for good reason.

Day trip: the Everglades

Roughly 45–60 minutes from Downtown, Everglades National Park is a completely different Florida—airboat tours, wildlife and a genuine break from the city. Worth a half or full day if you have more than a long weekend.

MOST CONVENIENT

South Beach, early morning

QUIETEST

Key Biscayne

SOMETHING DIFFERENT

Everglades day trip

THE WORTH-IT RULE: If you are staying in South Beach, do not drive to another beach—you are already at one. Save the trip to Key Biscayne or the Everglades for a day when the plan is specifically to get away from where you are staying.
