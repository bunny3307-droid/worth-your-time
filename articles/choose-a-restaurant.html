EAT · DECISION GUIDE

How to Choose a Restaurant in a Tourist City

A practical filter for finding a genuinely good meal when a neighborhood is full of places competing for your attention.

5

QUESTIONS BEFORE YOU BOOK.
What is the specialty? Who actually recommends it? Does the menu make sense for the neighborhood? What will the meal cost? And would you still choose it if you had never seen the restaurant online?

01 · Start with the reason to go

Great restaurants usually have a point of view. It might be a dish, a regional tradition, a style of cooking or a room that is genuinely part of the experience. “Everything is amazing” is not a useful reason to cross town.

02 · Read for specifics

Look for reviews and recommendations that mention what people ordered, how long they stayed, whether the service matched the room and whether the meal was worth the price. Specificity is a stronger signal than enthusiasm.

03 · Match the restaurant to the night

A destination restaurant can be worth the splurge for a birthday and completely wrong for a casual Tuesday. Decide what the meal needs to accomplish before comparing menus.

04 · Count the travel cost

If dinner requires an hour each way, a reservation window that forces you to rush and a ride home after, that is part of the price. Sometimes the slightly less famous restaurant nearby is the better experience.
