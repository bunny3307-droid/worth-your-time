How to Plan Your First International Trip.

The logistics matter more than the itinerary on a first international trip. Get these right first, then build the fun part.

Passport and visa timing

Check your passport's expiration date first—many countries require at least six months of validity remaining beyond your travel dates. Confirm visa requirements for your specific nationality and destination well ahead of booking; requirements vary widely and can take weeks to process.

Money that works abroad

Tell your bank and card issuer about your travel dates before you leave to avoid a fraud-related freeze. Carry at least two working payment methods, and confirm whether your destination leans cash-first or card-first—Seoul is largely card- and app-friendly; smaller vendors in Miami's neighborhood markets may prefer cash.

Phone and connectivity

An eSIM or a local SIM card is usually cheaper and more reliable than an international roaming plan for a trip longer than a few days. Set it up before you land, or know exactly where to get one on arrival.

Pace, not just a list

Your first international trip does not need to answer every question about a country in one visit. Build in slower days and unscheduled time—see our guide to planning without overplanning—rather than trying to see everything once.

Pick one anchor destination

Rather than splitting a first trip across too many cities, anchor it around one—our Miami and Seoul guides are both built this way—and go deep on neighborhoods instead of shallow across an entire country.
