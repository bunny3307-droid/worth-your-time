How to Find Good Coffee in Any City.

The same handful of heuristics work whether you are in Little Havana or Seongsu—you just have to know what to look for.

Look at who is inside, not just the sign

A café full of laptops and locals having a normal Tuesday is a stronger signal than an elaborate storefront. Tourist-trap cafés tend to cluster right next to major attractions; walk one or two blocks further and the quality-to-price ratio usually improves.

Ask what they are known for

Every good local coffee spot has an answer when you ask what to order—a specific bean, a specific drink, a specific roast style. A blank stare or a generic 'everything is good' is a weaker sign than a specific, confident answer.

Check the roast date, when you can see it

Specialty cafés that roast their own or work with a specific roaster will often have that information visible on the bag or the menu. It is not required, but its presence is usually a sign of a shop that cares about the details.

Let the city's own culture guide you

In cities with a strong café culture—Seoul's Seongsu district is the clearest example—cafés are a genuine local pastime, not just a caffeine stop, and the density of good options is high enough that wandering works. In cities without that culture, a little more research ahead of time pays off.

Do not skip the local specialty

A Cuban cafecito in Little Havana and a Korean dessert café in Seongsu are both, in their own way, the local coffee culture worth experiencing—not a detour from real coffee, but the actual point of drinking it somewhere new.

Locals with laptops, not just a queue of tourists

The local specialty drink

THE WORTH-IT RULE: If a café is on every top-ten list for the neighborhood, it is probably good—but so is the one two blocks away with no list at all. Use the lists as a starting point, not the final answer.
