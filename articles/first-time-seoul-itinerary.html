SEOUL · ITINERARY

First-Time Seoul: A Four-Day Itinerary That Clusters, Not Commutes.

A four-day shape built around neighborhood clusters, so you spend the trip exploring instead of crossing the city back and forth on the subway.

9.1

WORTH YOUR TIME.
Built for a first trip that wants culture, food, cafés and nightlife without over-scheduling any single day.

The rule this itinerary follows

Seoul is dense and well connected, which tempts people into stacking too much into one day. This plan keeps each day geographically tight. For the full case behind each area, read the neighborhoods guide first.

Day 1: Historic Seoul

Start at Gyeongbokgung Palace, ideally timed around one of the changing-of-the-guard ceremonies. Rent a hanbok nearby beforehand—it is a genuinely fun tradition and gets you free palace entry. Spend the afternoon in Bukchon Hanok Village and Insadong, and end with dinner in the same area rather than commuting elsewhere for the night.

Day 2: Seongsu + design

Give yourself the whole day to wander. Seongsu works best without a rigid schedule—browse the converted-warehouse cafés and concept stores, stop for bingsu in the afternoon, and let one shop or café lead to the next. See the café guide for what to look for.

Day 3: Hongdae + nightlife

Use the afternoon for shopping and cafés around Hongdae, then stay local for dinner and the night rather than commuting back and forth. If clubs are not your scene, swap in a Gangnam noraebang night instead—see the nightlife guide for the full breakdown, including how to get home after the subway stops.

Day 4: Gangnam or Itaewon + flexible time

Choose based on your mood: Gangnam for shopping and a modern skyline, Itaewon for food variety and an international crowd. Keep the day lighter than the rest so you have room for a repeat visit to a favorite spot from earlier in the trip.

THE WORTH-IT RULE: In Seoul, a nearby plan can beat a best-of-Seoul plan, because the city is so easy to keep exploring once you are already in the right area.
