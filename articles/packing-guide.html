A Packing Framework That Works for Any Trip.

The goal is not a longer packing list. It is a system that stops you from either overpacking or forgetting the thing you actually needed.

Start from the itinerary, not the closet

Pack for the specific things you have planned—the neighborhoods you will walk, the one nice dinner, the beach afternoon—rather than a generic idea of the destination. A detailed itinerary, like the ones in our city guides, makes this dramatically easier than packing from a blank list.

Layer instead of duplicating

A small number of pieces that combine in different ways beats a large number of single-use outfits. This matters most in cities with a real temperature swing between day and night, or between seasons like Seoul's.

The one-bag test

Before a trip of a week or less, try to fit everything into one bag you can carry yourself without help. If you cannot, you are usually not packing for the trip—you are packing for a worst-case version of it that will not happen.

Documents and money, twice

Keep a digital copy of your passport, ID and any reservation confirmations somewhere separate from the physical originals. Carry at least two ways to pay—a primary card and a backup—in case one is lost, declined or simply not accepted somewhere.

The adapter and battery check

Confirm your destination's plug type and voltage before you leave, not at the hotel. A portable charger earns its space on almost any trip that involves a full day of walking and using your phone for maps.
