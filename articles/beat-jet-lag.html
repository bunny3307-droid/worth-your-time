How to Beat Jet Lag, Practically.

You will not eliminate jet lag on a long-haul trip. You can meaningfully shorten it with a few unglamorous habits.

Start shifting before you fly

For a trip with a large time-zone change—like a flight from the US to Seoul—nudging your sleep and meal times by even thirty to sixty minutes a day in the days before departure gives your body a head start.

Set your watch early

Switch your phone and mindset to the destination's time zone as soon as you board. Eat and sleep according to that clock, not your origin time zone, for the rest of the trip.

Light is the strongest signal

Get outside in daylight at your destination as soon as you reasonably can, especially in the morning. Light exposure is the single strongest cue your body uses to reset its clock—much stronger than caffeine or sleep aids alone.

Go easy on alcohol in the air

Cabin air is already dehydrating, and alcohol makes both the dehydration and the jet lag worse. Water and a normal sleep schedule on the plane, matched to your destination's night, does more for you than a drink does.

Protect your first real day

Do not schedule your most demanding day for day one. Give yourself a lighter first day—see our Miami and Seoul itineraries for an example of what that looks like in practice—so a rough night of sleep does not wreck your best-planned day.

Shift your schedule a little, daily

ON THE PLANE

ON ARRIVAL

Get outside in daylight, fast

THE WORTH-IT RULE: The first day of a trip is not the day to prove how much you can pack in. Protect it, and the rest of the trip goes better.
